    The Training & Placement Cell

    A degree earns an interview; it does not by itself win the job. The Training & Placement Cell exists to close that gap. It works with students from the pre-final year onward — on aptitude, on communication, on how an interview is actually conducted — and with employers, so that the companies arriving on campus are ones our graduates are prepared for.

    The Cell is open to every student of the university, across all schools and programmes, and its training runs alongside the timetable rather than after it.

    What the Cell Does

    • {# Sized in the markup as well as the CSS: an

      Aptitude & Reasoning

      Regular classes in quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning and verbal ability — the three papers almost every campus test opens with — from the pre-final year onward.

    • Communication & Soft Skills

      Group discussion, presentation and business communication practice, run in small groups so every student speaks rather than watches.

    • Mock Interviews

      Technical and HR interviews conducted by faculty and by visiting professionals, with feedback given individually afterwards.

    • Résumé & Profile

      Workshops on writing a résumé that survives a screening filter, and on keeping a professional profile that a recruiter can find.

    • Internships

      Summer and semester internships arranged with partner organisations, so a student has real work on the record before final placement.

    • Industry Interface

      Guest sessions, industry-led certification courses and Board of Studies input, which is how the syllabus stays answerable to what employers actually ask for.

    How Campus Recruitment Runs

    {# The steps number themselves — insert an
  • anywhere and the rest #} {# renumber. Do not type the numbers into the text. #}

    1. Registration

      Eligible students register with the Cell at the start of the placement season and share an up-to-date résumé.

    2. Pre-placement talk

      The visiting company presents its role, its selection process and its terms, and takes questions from students.

    3. Written test

      An aptitude or technical test, usually online, sat on campus under the company's own supervision.

    4. Group discussion

      Held where the recruiter includes one, and used to shortlist for the interviews that follow.

    5. Technical & HR interviews

      One or more rounds, conducted by the company on campus or online, co-ordinated by the Cell.

    6. Offer

      Selected students receive their offer through the Cell, which keeps the record and follows up on joining.
